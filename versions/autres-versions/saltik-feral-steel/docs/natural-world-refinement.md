# Natural canyon routes — 23 October 2026

The three playable elevations now use individual, authored earth contours. Each of the six sections has different lengths, entry positions and elevation knots. `world.js` supplies the same `surfaceHeight(platform, x)` to tank support, enemy spawning and movement, obstacle placement, repair placement and terrain rendering. The ground also has broader, layered undulation. Upper contours are bounded 69–106 world units above their lower approach so ordinary jumps can reach them.

`scenery.js` replaces the metal catwalks, support struts, outlined rims and numbered route beacons. The paths sit on textured eroded rock, with deep irregular ends and unequal vaults. Substantial connected crags reach below ground; transparent, irregular ground and middle grottos cut through those masses. The narrow spans between the crags read as natural rock bridges. Moss, small grasses, ferns, shrubs, finer roots and hanging vines follow the actual surface. The generated cliff texture uses varied 620-unit crops rather than tiny repeated tiles.

The ruins plane uses three different transparent painted buildings: a station, observatory and communications ruin. Their actual unequal atlas bounds are cropped separately and grounded at their opaque bases. Sparse procedural foliage and stone plinths tie them into the existing canyon. There remain exactly eight world depth planes. Terrain, rock crags, paths and entities share the terrain plane; architecture stays on the ruins plane.

Jump animation contract: `player.jumpWindup` is **seconds remaining**, initially 0.085 and then decreasing to zero. The hull stays grounded during anticipation; launch occurs at expiry. A quick released press still launches, a held press launches only once, and an explicit descent cancels an elevated windup. `player.landingCompression` is normalized 0–1, set by the impact and decayed linearly over 0.18 seconds. The renderer delegates the tank to `tank-art.js` with these fields.

Camera framing starts immediately at the player's safe horizontal anchor, including negative world camera coordinates at the beginning. Small viewports use 35% of the screen; larger ones use 29%. The lower camera limit uses the player's legal x=70 boundary, so retreating to the start also keeps the tank clear of the directional pad.

Verification: new windup, landing, independent contour and entity anchoring tests were observed failing before their implementations. The complete 38-test suite passes. Ordinary movement and weapon inputs win the ground route in 80.43 seconds, with 40 armor, 46 kills and 24 destroyed obstacles. A second campaign climbs all six upper sections and wins in 80.43 seconds with the same remaining armor. Neither campaign uses teleportation or a countdown. Chromium screenshots were inspected during the first natural-art pass and again after adding connected crags and reducing oversized procedural foliage. Source and runtime build are updated; integration and publication belong to the parent task.

Very short jump/descend taps are queued by the input controller until one fixed simulation step consumes them. Held sources remain independent and pause clears queued edges along with held controls. Two additional regressions cover sub-frame release, held state and clearing.
